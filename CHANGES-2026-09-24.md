## Changed files

### `app/globals.css` — warm rebalance
The draft was built on blue-biased neutrals (`#f5f7f9`, `#eaeef2`, `#d9e0e6`) over a navy structural colour, so the site read blue even in its white sections. A marketer told EVO that blue is overused in the sector.

This is answered at token level rather than by changing the accent. The neutrals and the dark bands move to warm equivalents; **orange is untouched**; every page shifts together because nothing else in the stylesheet references a raw colour. The `--navy-*` variable names are kept so no component has to change — only the values are warm now.

To revert: restore the `:root` block from git history. One edit.

A block of new component styles is appended at the end of the file, prefixed `ev2-` so nothing can collide with the existing class library. Everything else on the new pages uses classes that were already there.

### `data/site.js` — navigation
- `mainNav`: nine top-level items become six. **Who we help returns to the nav** as a single tab with a three-item dropdown.
- `footerNav`: the six sector links become three; a link to the new Trades page is added under Residents.
- `Damp & mould` is deliberately left in the nav. Patch 2 replaces it with `Compliance`, with damp and mould underneath. Leaving it means the nav never points at a page that does not exist yet.

**Why Who we help went back on the nav.** It was taken off when the drafts were duplicates — 1,054 and 1,058 words with the same eight sections in the same order — and demoting a duplicate costs nothing. Writing the pages out properly changed the evidence: the housing page carries seven blocks that appear nowhere else on the site, most of them what a tender actually scores (social value, frameworks, health and safety, net zero, HMS integration). Reaching that only through a homepage tile is the wrong place for it.

One page with accordions was considered and rejected. Not for the usual reason — Google now treats accordion content as a normal pattern and gives it full ranking weight when it is in the HTML at load. The reason is that one URL has one title tag, one H1 and one slug, and cannot be pointed at three different search intents. Three URLs can.

### `next.config.mjs` — redirects
Six old sector URLs 301 to the three new ones, plus `/sectors` itself and the five old `/pricing/*-plans` URLs that currently have nowhere to land.

## Two things deliberately withheld

**Contractor rate card figures.** The Trades page says a rate card exists and that materials are paid with an uplift. The rates, the uplift percentages and the abortive attendance fee are **not published**. They are EVO's cost base: publishing them tells clients the margin, tells competitors the cost structure, and anchors every future contractor negotiation.

**Emergency routing on the Residents page.** Emergencies go through the app and are actioned immediately. The phone number is a fallback below a divider, not the headline action. The only place the page sends a resident away from EVO is gas (0800 111 999) and fire (999), which is correct and protects EVO as much as the resident.

# Patch 2 — homepage and Compliance

Added after Sam pointed out, correctly, that the deployed site still looked like the original: patch 1 changed five pages and the palette, and left the homepage — the page you see first — untouched.

**`npm run build` passes: 60 static pages, no errors.**

## `app/page.jsx` — homepage rebuilt to the agreed mockup

- **Hero**: the headline emphasises *fixed-price*, the combined award badge image is replaced by two text lines each naming the client the award was won with (GLOBAL-06), and the laptop-and-phone render is replaced by a photograph of a resident using the Living App, with a caption chip.
- **"Why repairs matter more than ever"** is now its own band, not part of "the problem". It sets the stakes before anyone is told they have a problem, which is the right order for a housing director.
- **Problem and answer line up row by row** — four pairs with an icon between them, copy taken from the brochure so the site and the brochure say the same words. This replaces the four numbered solution points, which did the same job in twice the space.
- **The Caretaker** gets its own block with a photograph. It is the one differentiator no platform competitor can answer.
- **Video testimonials move down** into the proof block. They were second on the page, above the problem and the solution, so a first-time visitor was asked to watch a video before knowing what EVO is.
- **The compliance split** — statutory work we do, regulatory duty that stays with the client — on a dark band.
- **A thin "who we help" strip** rather than a section, since the sector pages are in the nav now.
- **Trust block** leading with Kate Davies and Steve Norris, which the old 123-word Who we are section never mentioned.
- **See it / Try it / Start it** merges the old pilot section and closing CTA, which were two sections making the same offer in different words.
- **The founder quote is removed from the homepage.** Mark explaining why EVO exists is an About question, not a buying one. It needs adding to `/about` when that page is rebuilt.

New components: `WhyNow`, `ProblemAnswer`, `Icons2`, `Caretaker`, `ComplianceBand`, `WhoStrip`, `TrustBlock`, `GettingStarted`.

New photographs: `evo-resident-living-app.webp`, `evo-caretaker-on-estate.webp`.

## `app/compliance/page.jsx` — new page

Takes the nav slot, with damp and mould underneath it. Splits statutory (work EVO does) from regulatory (a duty that never transfers), then the four regulatory pillars, what gets recorded and when — including no-access, the record landlords most often cannot produce — and an explicit section on what EVO does *not* take on. That last one matters: the wrong claim here would leave a client exposed rather than covered.
