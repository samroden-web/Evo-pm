## Changed files

### `app/globals.css` — warm rebalance
The draft was built on blue-biased neutrals (`#f5f7f9`, `#eaeef2`, `#d9e0e6`) over a navy structural colour, so the site read blue even in its white sections. A marketer told EVO that blue is overused in the sector.

This is answered at token level rather than by changing the accent. The neutrals and the dark bands move to warm equivalents; **orange is untouched**; every page shifts together because nothing else in the stylesheet references a raw colour. The `--navy-*` variable names are kept so no component has to change — only the values are warm now.

To revert: restore the `:root` block from git history. One edit.

A block of new component styles is appended at the end of the file, prefixed `ev2-` so nothing can collide with the existing class library. Everything else on the new pages uses classes that were already there.

### `data/site.js` — navigation
- `mainNav`: nine top-level items become six. **Who we help returns to the nav** as a single tab with a three-item dropdown.
- `footerNav`: the six sector links become three; a link to the new Trades page is added under Residents.
- `Damp & mould` is deliberately left in the nav. Patch 2 replaces it with `Compliance`, with damp and mould underneath. Leaving it means the nav never points at a page that does not exist yet.

**Why Who we help went back on the nav.** It was taken off when the drafts were duplicates — 1,054 and 1,058 words with the same eight sections in the same order — and demoting a duplicate costs nothing. Writing the pages out properly changed the evidence: the housing page carries seven blocks that appear nowhere else on the site, most of them what a tender actually scores (social value, frameworks, health and safety, net zero, HMS integration). Reaching that only through a homepage tile is the wrong place for it.

One page with accordions was considered and rejected. Not for the usual reason — Google now treats accordion content as a normal pattern and gives it full ranking weight when it is in the HTML at load. The reason is that one URL has one title tag, one H1 and one slug, and cannot be pointed at three different search intents. Three URLs can.

### `next.config.mjs` — redirects
Six old sector URLs 301 to the three new ones, plus `/sectors` itself and the five old `/pricing/*-plans` URLs that currently have nowhere to land.

`cleared: false` stays. Written sign-off for web use is a separate, still-open item.

# Patch 3 — the brand, properly

## The palette, written down this time

Treatment B, as agreed on the homepage mockup swatch strip: *"Orange leads, navy is
text only, and the cold greys are replaced with warm ones. Three full orange bands
break up the page: the figures, the pilot and the close."* Three rules follow, and they
are now in a comment at the top of `globals.css` so the next person does not have to
reconstruct them:

1. **Orange leads.** It is a field colour, not a highlighter.
2. **Navy is text only**, and it stays cool — `#2b3a47` type, `#1c2833` dark bands. An
   earlier pass warmed navy to brown; that was wrong. Warm brown type on warm grey has
   no separation and reads muddy rather than warm.
3. **The greys are warm** — `#fbf8f5 / #f3ede7 / #e6dcd3`. This is what takes the blue
   cast out of the white sections without touching the type colour.

Statutory is work EVO actually does, so it gets the solid colour. Regulatory is a duty

## One deliberate departure from the mockup: contrast

The mockups put white text on `#ff6600`. That is 2.9:1, and AA needs 4.5:1 for anything
under 18.66px bold — so on a live site it fails, and it fails for exactly the readers
who need it most.

Rather than deepen the orange (which would lose the thing Sam asked for), the bands stay
bright and the *small* text on them goes dark: `--on-orange: #3a1c00` at 5.3:1. Large
headings and the big figures stay white. White numerals with dark captions on bright
orange is a stronger piece of design than white-on-white-on-orange anyway.

Two tokens moved a shade darker for the same reason and are visually indistinguishable:
`--orange-ink` `#c24a00` → `#b34700`, `--muted` `#6b7a87` → `#5d6b78`. Both now clear AA
on white, on `--grey-50` and on `--grey-100`.

## 4. Two things that need a command run, and why

The build environment could fetch pages but **not binary files**, so no image could be
downloaded automatically. Two scripts do it from anywhere with normal internet access:

```
bash tools/fetch-team-photos.sh      # 29 team photographs
bash tools/fetch-article-images.sh   # 47 article cover images
```

Each one downloads the files and then runs a small linker
(`tools/link-team-photos.mjs`, `tools/link-article-images.mjs`) that sets the image paths
**from what actually landed on disk**. That is deliberate: the data files hold `null` until
the file genuinely exists, so the repo can never point at an image that has not been
downloaded. A team member without a photograph shows their initials on a warm tile, which
reads as deliberate; an article without a cover shows the existing navy EVO placeholder.

Sixteen of the older articles have no cover image on the live site either. Nothing to fetch
for those.

## 5. Newsletters — the archive is broken on the live site

All 34 newsletter pages on the live site return a **500 server error**. Confirmed two
ways: three different issues were fetched and all three errored, and Sam opened one in a
browser and saw a title and a photograph but **no newsletter**. That is the signature of a
template failing server-side while the page shell still renders — so the archive is broken
today, independently of the rebuild, and a crawler asking for any of those 34 URLs gets an
error rather than a page.

Which means there is nothing to migrate and no search value to preserve. What the rebuild
does instead:

- **`/insights/newsletters` is now a real archive page**, listing all 34 issues by number
  and month, grouped by year, with the sign-up alongside.
- **Every old issue URL 301s to it** via a single wildcard rule, so nothing 404s and any
  link a reader has saved still lands somewhere sensible.
- **`data/newsletters.js` keeps the record** — number, month, date and original slug for
  every issue, so nothing is quietly lost.

**Recovering the content.** The sign-up form on the site is a Mailchimp embed, so the
newsletters were emailed through Mailchimp and the original copy will be in its campaign
archive. Add `body` to an entry in `data/newsletters.js` and set `restored: true`, and
that issue becomes a page of its own at `/insights/newsletters/<slug>` with a link from
the list. The page is built to fill up gradually rather than needing all 34 at once.

Worth telling whoever runs the current site that the archive is erroring, regardless of
what happens with the rebuild.

# Patch 6 — the resident video, and seven testimonials that were on the bench

## 1. The resident vox pop reel

EVO has one video: **2 minutes 19 seconds, shot vertical at 1080×1920, 60fps, 512MB** —
residents in their own kitchens and doorways talking about reporting a repair on the
Living App, with EVO title cards and a music track.

The build was carrying **three empty landscape slots marked TBC**, per the original brief,

**It is served from the site, not Vimeo.** The 512MB master compresses to **11MB** at

**Why it sits where it does.** The written testimonials are from landlords and managing
agents — the buyer's peers. This is the buyer's *residents*, and resident satisfaction is

the one part of a supplier's pitch a buyer can check for themselves.

**Consent.** The residents are identifiable. EVO produced this as a finished marketing
reel, so consent was presumably taken at filming — worth confirming once before launch,

# Patch 13 — an automated pre-deploy check, and two gaps research turned up

Not a design change. This is the bit that should have existed from patch one.

## tools/check-site.mjs

Crawls every URL in the sitemap against a running build. `npm run check`.

It **fails** the run on: any non-200, a missing or duplicated H1, a missing or duplicated
title or meta description, a broken local image, or any string in its `BANNED_TEXT` list.
It **warns** on over-length titles and descriptions, and prints every visible TBC tag.

First run: **102 pages, 73 images, no failures.** 32 over-length article titles and 20
over-length descriptions as warnings — those are the migrated originals, so leaving them
is an editorial choice rather than a defect. 29 TBC tags listed, all of them real.

Every check corresponds to something that actually shipped on this project and was caught
by eye afterwards, which is the point — three times I reported something fixed that was
only fixed on the page I happened to be looking at. `BANNED_TEXT` is the part worth
maintaining: it holds retired claims and the cost-base figures, so **the check remembers
what must never come back**. Add a line whenever a claim is retired. It currently blocks
the 28-plus sector comparison, the 35–60% savings claim, the 15p-a-day line, the 23%
compliance claim, the 25% variable-works uplift, the out-of-hours uplifts and the abortive
fee.

It exits non-zero, so it can go straight into a GitHub Action later.

## Security headers

`next.config.mjs` now sets `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy`, `X-Frame-Options`,
`Permissions-Policy` and `Strict-Transport-Security`. All verified present on a local
build. A company selling compliance should not be missing the cheap ones, and a
procurement security questionnaire asks about them by name.

**A Content-Security-Policy is deliberately not included.** Next.js needs a nonce for its
inline bootstrap script, and a CSP written without one breaks the page for real visitors
while looking perfectly fine in a build. It is worth doing as its own change, with the
report-only header first.

## The finding that matters most, and it is a legal one

The privacy policy we migrated states that **EVO uses Google Analytics**. The new site
loads **no analytics at all** and has **no cookie banner**.

So one of two things is true: the policy overstates what the site does, or the moment
anyone adds analytics the site is non-compliant. Under UK PECR as amended by the **Data
(Use and Access) Act 2025**, analytics cookies need prior opt-in consent, "reject" must be
as prominent as "accept", nothing may fire before consent is given, and the penalty
ceiling rose from £500,000 to **£17.5m or 4% of turnover** — matching UK GDPR. The ICO now
tests by rejecting everything and checking nothing fires.

Two options, both cheap today and expensive later:

1. **No analytics.** Correct the privacy policy to match, and delete the Google Analytics
   paragraph. Nothing else to do.
2. **Analytics behind a consent gate.** A banner with equal-prominence accept and reject,
   no pre-ticked boxes, scripts blocked until consent, and the choice logged.

Doing nothing is the one option that does not work, because the policy already claims
option 2 while the site is built like option 1.

## Also worth knowing

- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA is the stated target and an accessibility statement is
  still outstanding. Colour contrast was computed when the palette was set, but keyboard
  focus order, alt text quality and form labelling have never been audited end to end.
  Worth one proper pass before launch.
- **The DNS cutover** is the next genuinely risky moment. Every image must be in the repo
  before it, the 301s want re-testing, and somebody should watch 404s for a week after.
  The old site's media becomes unrecoverable the moment the domain moves.

# Patch 14 — How it works, 25 September

## 1. The orange band

Four blocks that were all one weight — dark ink heading, dark ink body, a thin white rule
— so there was no entry point for the eye. Each block now leads with a large white numeral.

The numeral is doing contrast work as well as design work. **Measured on #ff6600: white is
2.94:1, `--on-orange` (#3a1c00) is 5.31:1, `--on-orange-soft` (#4a2400) is 4.64:1.** So no
*text* in the band can be white. The numeral can, because it is decorative: it is
`aria-hidden`, the four points have no inherent order, and it carries nothing a reader
needs. Headings take the darkest ink, body text the slightly lighter one, which is the
hierarchy step the band was missing.

`.ev2-band-item h3` was **defined twice**, in two places, with different colours. Now once.

# Patch 18 — phones, photographs and the logo strip, 26 September

## 2. The logos (patch 27, folded in)

`tools/fetch-client-logos.sh` deleted committed files on a failed download: `curl -o`
truncates the destination before the transfer starts, and the failure branch then ran
`rm -f "$2"`. The deploy block re-runs every fetch on every patch, so the first time
evo-pm.com stopped serving one of those `/media/` URLs, the good committed copies were
wiped, `withFiles` filtered the logos out, and they vanished from the homepage **silently**.
No error, no failed build.

Now: download to a temp file, move on success only, never touch the committed copy on
failure. Same fix applied to the article and team-photo scripts (76 downloads between them).
`link-client-logos.mjs` prints a banner when the strip is short, and **refuses to write at
all** when not one file is found — running it here had rewritten `data/logos.js` with every
logo cleared, which would have shipped a homepage with no client strip.
